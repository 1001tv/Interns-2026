One of the main objectives of the internship was to expose us to different areas of technology instead of placing everyone into one role from the beginning. We were supposed to work on different topics and tasks so we could get a better idea of the paths available in the field and actually try some of them for ourselves.

As the internship progressed, we started focusing more on the areas that suited us best. For me, that became backend development. This gave me the chance to go beyond briefly learning about different topics and actually spend more time building my skills in one area.

My own goal was to get some real experience and understand what it would actually feel like to work in a tech environment. I wanted to know if this was something I would genuinely enjoy doing in the future because, before the internship, most of what I knew felt very theoretical. By the end of the internship, I had a much better idea of what I enjoyed working on, and I felt more motivated to continue working on and improving both my technical and soft skills.

## Summary of Completed Tasks

During the internship, I worked on several different tasks. First, we started with version control, where I learned more about Git and GitHub and how they are used when working on projects. After that, I prepared a full report on the 1001.tv application and website, and then moved into one of my favorite parts, which was Android development. We created an application without writing code, and I recreated a recipe app using Kodular. I had so much fun working on it, and I genuinely enjoyed the whole process.

Later, I moved into backend development and started with P0, where I built a basic Users API using TypeScript and Express. I worked with routes, requests and responses, validation, HTTP status codes, error handling, and testing. This task helped me understand the basics of how a backend works before moving on to something bigger. At that point, I hadn't even worked on connecting a proper database yet.

For P1, the project became much more advanced. We started recreating parts of the 1001 application from scratch as a team, with me working on the backend. I worked on PostgreSQL and Sequelize, registration and login, password hashing, JWT authentication, user profiles, password recovery, favorites, and connecting the backend to TMDB for movie and TV data. I also worked closely with the frontend side by making the backend accessible to the team and preparing a simple reference of the endpoints so they would be easier to use.

Throughout the internship, I also submitted weekly reports where I documented what I worked on, what I learned, the challenges I faced, and my progress each week. By the end of the internship, I had gone from working on smaller introductory tasks to building and submitting a much more complete backend project.

## Main Challenges and Solutions

One of my biggest challenges during the internship was getting past the feeling that I understood something in theory but did not really know how to use it. This happened a lot when I first moved into backend development. There were many concepts I had studied before, especially databases, but actually building something with them felt very different. What helped me was slowing down, focusing on one thing at a time, and testing small parts until I understood how everything connected.

Another challenge was that the project requirements sometimes changed or became clearer through meetings rather than only through the written task. I had also missed a few meetings because of personal circumstances, so there were times when I had to catch up and understand decisions that had already been made. Instead of guessing, I started asking more questions, checking with the team, and making sure I understood what the frontend needed before continuing with my part.

I also ran into a lot of technical problems along the way. Sometimes it was Git, VS Code, dependencies, migrations, or even my laptop, and other times it was something completely outside the code. One example was when my backend worked perfectly on my own laptop, but Bashar could not connect to it from another device. I had to check the port, ZeroTier, firewall settings, and the network profile before finally getting it to work.

Outside of the technical side, I was also dealing with a lot personally and academically during the internship. One of the hardest parts was the timing, especially when I had several midterms and other responsibilities happening at the same time. There were points where everything felt very overwhelming, and keeping up with university while also staying on top of the internship was difficult. I am really thankful that Amnah was supportive and understanding during those times, which made it much easier to communicate when I was struggling.

Even when I fell behind or felt overwhelmed, I still kept working, even if I had to move at a slower pace. I continued giving my updates and tried to find a way to catch up and finish the work I was responsible for. I think that taught me a lot about responsibility and not giving up just because things became difficult.

Overall, one of the biggest things I learned from these challenges was to stop panicking when something did not work immediately. At the beginning, I would sometimes feel completely stuck, but over time I became better at troubleshooting one thing at a time, asking for help when I needed it, and not being afraid to go back and fix something I had misunderstood.
